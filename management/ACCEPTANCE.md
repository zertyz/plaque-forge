# Homologation and reconstruction

**Freeze what the human approved. Measure whether current software can reproduce it.** A reference may be imperfect; an unapproved deviation from it is not improvement.

## A-001 Approved baseline

A homologation is a versioned, human-approved package, not a generated cache.

| Record | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Source and render inputs | Exact media, timeline/color interpretation, text, font, style, and relevant render settings |
| Human inputs | The approved correction set, assistance classification, provenance, and correction-effort record |
| Resolved scene | Complete data for every frame and affected pixel: geometry/deformation, writing regions, visibility, depth, masks/alpha, material, and any reconstructed source content |
| Approved rendering | Lossless reference frames with timing/color metadata, plus identities of reviewed delivery artifacts |
| Acceptance | Per-dimension quality findings, known imperfections, independent witnesses, evaluator versions/configuration, reviewer, date, and approval |

The resolved scene must be sufficient for the renderer without discovery, tracking, segmentation, or other analysis. Reproducible compression/representation is allowed; missing information cannot be regenerated by silently invoking current analysis. The human approves generated detailed data rather than authoring it pixel by pixel.

Bind all consumed inputs by identity. Preserve accepted packages outside disposable cache/reset/cleanup paths. Versioned format migrations must prove lossless meaning and preserve the original evidence.

## A-002 Frozen replay

Render the approved resolved scene using the current renderer and the same render inputs. Compare every frame against the approved rendering.

This isolates rendering, compositing, typography, encoding, and dependency regressions from analysis changes. Reading the old rendered video and returning it as the new result is not renderer validation.

Different requested text/style has different expected pixels. Use an explicitly identified render case plus scene invariants; do not compare unrelated outputs as if their render inputs matched.

Acceptance: analysis-independent replay, complete frame coverage, fidelity under A-004, and exact identities under A-006.

## A-003 Reconstruction and remapping

Report these lanes separately:

| Lane | Inputs | Question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Automatic | Source and requested operation/text/style; no correction annotations or frozen answers | What can current analysis establish unaided? |
| Existing assistance | Source plus the unchanged approved human input set | Can current analysis still reconstruct the accepted result at the previous correction burden? |
| Proposed remapping | Source plus a separately recorded candidate correction set | Can changed analysis regain accepted quality with comparable or less human effort? |

An automatically homologated case requires automatic reconstruction of its target. An assisted case requires reconstruction with its approved assistance; its automatic lane measures progress without claiming the full target was already achievable unaided. Independently accepted automatic-stage constraints remain binding.

Fresh reconstruction cannot reuse the frozen resolved scene, old per-video analysis answers, or oracle-derived corrections as automatic results. Ordinary runtime/model caches may be reused only when they do not supply the answer being evaluated.

Always retain the existing-input result, including its failures. Remapping does not erase it. A remapping can be accepted as evolution when quality is preserved and effort is comparable or lower; substantially increased correction is a regression even if other examples improve.

An approved remapping creates a new input version. Keep the visual target unchanged when quality is unchanged; changed approved pixels require a successor homologation. Until approval, a proposed remapping cannot turn a failed required case green.

Acceptance: independent execution/provenance for each lane, quality comparison, correction-effort evidence, and human promotion where inputs or targets change.

## A-004 Quality and fidelity

Assess two separate properties:

- **Intrinsic quality:** how well the approved or proposed result achieves the human's visual intention. Record imperfections rather than hiding them in an overall grade.
- **Fidelity:** whether the current result reproduces the approved result for the same case. Known baseline imperfections do not permit additional drift.

Evaluate placement/shape/deformation, temporal attachment and visibility, depth/occlusion, fine detail and alpha, typography/readability, and source/color/timeline preservation. Report per-frame failures and localized visual differences; a good average cannot compensate for a failed dimension or a bad interval.

Retain lossless pixel evidence and inspect every frame. Compare within a declared color/timing pipeline. Exact hashes identify artifacts; differing container or encoder bytes alone do not establish visual drift.

Calibrate numerical-noise allowances with controlled repeat renders and deliberately introduced visible defects. Such allowances must not permit visible changes. Review and protect comparator configuration under G-002; never widen it to pass a candidate. Numerical criteria require measured evidence, not arbitrary percentages.

A proposed correction of a baseline defect is evaluated as an improvement proposal, with BEFORE/AFTER evidence and human judgment. It cannot silently replace the reference.

Acceptance: calibrated evaluators reject demonstrated defects and local/temporal drift; uncertain comparisons remain unresolved, not passing.

## A-005 Human correction effort

Record what was corrected: selected surfaces, prompts/anchors, corrected objects and frames, authored masks/trajectories, semantic constraints, interactions, and measured correction time when available. Separate authored work from machine-generated expansion and proposed automated remapping.

Compare against both the active input version and its earlier approved burden to expose gradual growth. Reuse of old approved answers is assistance, even when copying it takes one click.

Do not equate file size, annotation count, or an invented “99% automatic” score with human effort. Use the concrete correction record and reproducible tasks; label unknown historical effort. Human review decides comparability when dimensions disagree.

Acceptance: preserved quality with comparable/lower burden, explicit increases, and no trade of one video's substantial regression for another's improvement.

## A-006 Trustworthy evaluation

The candidate implementation cannot generate its own acceptance oracle or alter the evaluator's protected expectations. Diagnostic traces explain causes; they complement independent evidence.

Challenge checks with missing titles, wrong geometry/motion, broken occlusion, opaque gaps, color/timing errors, frozen-output substitution, leaked analysis answers, stale identities, and omitted frames/cases. Record the affected requirement for every deliberate fault.

Reports identify the tested revision, source, baseline, human input version, runtime/models, render inputs, delivered artifact, evaluator, command, and outcome. Certifying a lossless intermediate does not certify a later delivery transcode.

Acceptance: adversarial checks fail for the right reason; missing, skipped, stale, or uncertain evidence cannot pass.

## A-007 Establishment and promotion

1. Try automatic analysis; let the human inspect a trial render.
2. Add corrections only when needed; record their role and burden.
3. Capture the resolved scene, lossless rendering, independent witnesses, and quality assessment.
4. Human approval establishes the versioned baseline.
5. Later changes run replay and reconstruction; proposals show BEFORE/AFTER quality and effort.

Review all supplied examples under this protocol. Keep the eight existing recorded contracts protecting their accepted behavior until human-reviewed successors replace them. Neither current caches nor previews automatically approve the other examples.

Every accepted contract is required before an output-affecting merge under [O-005](OPERATIONS.md#o-005-required-validation-coverage). As the new protocol is implemented, its accepted replay and reconstruction cases join that protection; existing coverage gaps remain visible until fixed.
