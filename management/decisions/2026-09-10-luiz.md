Q-003: Support separately assessed automatic/assisted modes, automatic-only analysis, or assisted analysis as the product?
D-003: We should support all of these. The "assessed automatic" should be the first thing done when a new video lands. Human will, then, do a trial render and see if additional info is required -- what you are calling as "assisted mode", I blelieve? In any case, the human must say "this automatic assessed scene was good enough -- it can be written in stone and the video homologation should check similar (or better) results are produced when the automatic assessment evolves". Same thing goes for the human provided data -- aka, "assisted mode"? -- The human, when just determined the automatic assessed info was not enough, produced additional info to bring the quality up and, after that, had the result written in stone as "this is the base quality for this video". As the project evolves, for sure the assisted (human given) information is applied on top of the automatic assessed info. So evolutions to the software may break homologated automatic & assisted info. There should be a clear way of "rendering" (pixel by pixel, frame by frame) the results so what is "written in stone" is not subject to any analysis changes; and -- also -- a way of always trying to make the automatic & assisted info to get to those "rendered" pixels. This is also plays a key factor in why this software was not progressing. A misshandling of what the "acceptable quality" / "homologated data" for a video meant VERSUS "are we still able to reconstruct that same acceptable quality for a previously homologated video"? Do you see the point here? On version 10 we may have gotten video A as "perfect" and saved the "raw detailed homologated data". Lets say that was a combination of automatic scene detection (99%) plus a few human inputs to that scene, which made the video A to reach the approved quality goal. Then, in version 50, we could no longer use the new automatic scene detection to reach 99% of the work -- what would result in the few human inputs useless. We can treat this as a regression -- which it is. AI / agents are required to reason about this situation and judge the following: "the change to the algorithm did change the automatic scene detection outcomes in a way that the automatic + assisted inputs now produce a rendered output in a lower quality than the homologated one" the resoning comes next: "Are there a few changes we need to do to the human provided data for that video that would bring the quality back to the homologated levels? Or we'd have to increase substantially the amount of human-fine-tunes needed to bring the renderings for that video back in shape"? One of those are to be considered a regression and denied; the other is to be considered an evolution, even if the "human provided data requires tweeking". The key here is to judge: "now the human-provided-fine-tuned data needed is at a greater scale than before or at a similar or, preferably, smaller scale"? By following these guidelines, our software goes forward and the amount of "fine-tunes/human-provided data" tends to diminish over time. Do you agree? Also, don't forget that for this whole thing to work, we should have a strong evaluator of "how good -- or how better or how worse -- the renderings are to the homologated one". We must keep in mind that an homologated rendering may not be perfect. So you might come up with a way to give a "quality assessment to the homologation". Question to you: do you agree with me that those changes are essential for the project to be always moving forward? To introduce the raw data on how to render the videos (every pixel dimentions, frame-by-frame) and call this our "homologated scene data" and give it a grade -- possibly over several dimentions? Of course the human won't build such detailed raw data. But the human will homologate it. It should not be changed unless we are doing better in the future. Even so, agents should never change it blindly: they should propose: "Look at this current rendering called BEFORE: with X complexity solved by the automatic algorithm + Y complexity required from human refined inputs; now look at this new rendering called AFTER: with an improved X' complexity solved by the automatic algorithm + an improved Y' complexity required from the human -- which we already have re-mapped... tell me if the new proposed homologation data (which was calculated to be better in the W, and Z dimentions by this and that amount) are indeed better". Do you see a whole new set of requirements deriving from this answer? Didn't know if you have though of that... they are for sure not included in the current POC. Just include them if you do agree with my arguments. Please followup if you have a batter alternative.
